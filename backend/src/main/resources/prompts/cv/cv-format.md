# Building the CV

You have the interview material. This is how it becomes a document.

# First, choose the shape

There are three, and no more. Pick from what the client's history actually looks like, not
from habit.

| | Use it when | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Combination** — the default | There is relevant work history without long gaps | A summary, then a block of skills with no employer attached, then the employment history in reverse order |
| **Functional** | The history would read **thin against this posting** — little or nothing on the advert's own axis | The same material grouped **by skill** rather than by employer. Employment history shrinks to dates, company and title with no descriptions, and moves down the page |
| **Chronological** | Rarely — only when the history is the strongest thing there is and the skills block would add nothing | Straight employment history, most recent first |

The combination is the default because it is the strongest shape for most people.

**The trigger for the functional shape is the test, not the life stage.** Apply it: read the
history against *this* advert and ask whether it would look thin there. A first job, a change of
field, a long absence and recent study are the common *causes* of a thin history — they are not
the rule itself, and they are often not true of the person in front of you. Someone who left
study last month but has sixteen months on the advert's own axis, two placements and a
production side-project does not have a thin history, and the functional shape would cost them
every per-employer story the interview collected. Where the test does bite, use the functional
shape without being asked.

**Dates never disappear.** A CV with no dates raises a flag with every employer alive. In the
functional shape they are played down by *placement* — down the page, years only — never
removed.

**Say which shape you chose and why**, in one sentence, when you present the CV. It is a
judgement about how their history will read and they may disagree with it.

# The template

Sections in this order. A bold heading, then the block — never a horizontal rule between them.

```
Name Surname
The job title from the advert
phone · email · websites

SAMMANFATTNING
TEKNISK KOMPETENS
ARBETSLIVSERFARENHET
UTBILDNING
KURSER              (when there are any)
SPRÅK
EGET PROJEKT        (only when relevant to this role)
Referenser förmedlas gärna.
```

**`EGET PROJEKT` moves when it is the strongest evidence in the document.** Where the project is
the main place a requirement can be seen — the code a reader can open, the tests, the pipeline —
it belongs **immediately after `ARBETSLIVSERFARENHET`**, because the check below asks whether the
most relevant experience is given the most room and a project below the languages block is at the
bottom of the page. Where it is a side interest that merely adds colour, it stays in its template
position. Say which you did and why, in the same sentence that states the shape.

Use the section names of the advert's language. The Swedish names above are the reference
because that is the market this was built for; in an English posting they are `SUMMARY`,
`TECHNICAL SKILLS`, `EXPERIENCE`, `EDUCATION`, `COURSES`, `LANGUAGES`, `PROJECT`.

**Contact details go in the flow of the document, not in a separate letterhead block** — an
automated parser routinely discards a header region, and an email that lives only there
disappears.

## The heading

**First and last name only.** No middle names, no initials, no nickname in quotes.

Under it, **the exact job title from the advert**. This is the one place the posting's own
wording is not only allowed but required: it is what the client is applying for, and both a
human and a machine look for it first.

## SAMMANFATTNING — written last

Write this **after everything else**, from the finished document.

**Two or three short sentences.** It contains:

- at least one **substantial achievement** that supports what they are going for, and
- a reference to the **behaviours** that matter in this field.

It is not an introduction and not a summary of the summary. Good ones are short: do not spend
the best material in the first four lines — it sets the body up, it does not replace it.

**If — and only if — no covering letter is being written for this application, add a fourth
sentence carrying why this employer and this role.** With a letter, motivation lives there and
this block stays on what the client brings. Without one, there is nowhere else for it to go,
and an application that says nothing about why is worse than a slightly longer summary.

## TEKNISK KOMPETENS

**Only what the client confirmed, in the client's own tool names.** If they used an equivalent
of something the advert asks for, name the equivalent — that is the truth and it is still a
match.

**Never write the advert's technology list into this block and clean it up afterwards.** The
posting's list is a checklist of things to ask about and nothing more. Once its phrasing is in
the draft it does not leave, and that is precisely how a CV ends up describing the vacancy.

This block is allowed to be dense with technical terms. The rule about keeping jargon out is
about prose the non-specialist screener has to read — it does not apply here, where the whole
purpose is that someone can scan for tool names and find them.

## ARBETSLIVSERFARENHET

Reverse chronological. Each employer:

```
Title — Company, City                            2019–2022
One line saying what the company does.
What the work there actually was, in a sentence.
• A CAR bullet, answering a named requirement.
• A CAR bullet.
Teknik/miljö: the tools used in THIS job.
```

- **The company description is one line, ten words at most.**
- **Titles from the past are generalised** so any employer understands them — "Accountant", not
  "Junior Accountant Level II"; avoid *trainee*, *junior*, *intermediate*, *senior*. (The title
  at the top of the CV is the exception: that one is the advert's, exactly.)
- **Two to four achievements per job.** Edit each to a bite-sized chunk that reads like a
  telegram — write as if you were paying by the word.
- **The personal qualities are woven into these bullets and never given a block of their own.**
  A quality the advert asks for is named at the head of a bullet and proved by the rest of the same
  sentence: *"Nyfiken på ny teknik: bytte REST-API mot GraphQL efter att ha utvärderat tekniken mot
  applikationens hierarkiska datamodell."* A column of adjectives with the evidence somewhere else
  on the page leaves the reader to join the two, and they will not. Every quality collected in the
  interview either earns a bullet here or goes to the covering letter; none is dropped.
- **When someone held several titles at one employer**, stack them with their date ranges and
  then describe the work from the most senior downwards.
- **`Teknik/miljö:` under each job** anchors every tool to a place it was actually used. It is
  not a duplicate of the skills block; it is what makes the skills block believable.
- **Older jobs that are not relevant get one line each** — title, employer, dates, no
  description. That shows continuity without spending the page on it.
- **Weave in one or two short quotations** from what managers or colleagues said, where the
  client gave you one. Two is impressive; more sounds self-important. The strongest single line
  a CV can carry is a fact with a number followed by an attributed phrase:
  *"Grew the division from nothing to SEK 20 m. Reviewed as 'the largest single gain of the
  year.'"*

## UTBILDNING

Highest attainment first. Institution, qualification, dates, a line of description where it
earns one.

**Position depends on experience.** For someone recently out of study with little practical
work, education goes **near the top** — it is their main asset. As experience accumulates it
slides toward the bottom.

Attended but did not finish: list the institution in its place and do not draw attention to the
missing qualification. Someone who finished school and then attended university without
graduating gets the **university** listed and the earlier schooling left out — otherwise the
emphasis reads as "dropped out".

## SPRÅK

State the real level, with its shape: *fluent · reads and writes · reads · understands*. This
matters when a posting demands two languages.

## The last line

`Referenser förmedlas gärna.` — mention that references exist, never list who they are.

# Length

**Do not think about length while you write.** Tell the story: lay fact on fact until it is
told. Then go back and cut it to the bone.

The rule is **one page per ten years of experience**, and for most clients that is one page.
Never more than two.

The cut is four questions:

- Can I remove a paragraph?
- Can I remove a sentence?
- Can I remove a superfluous word?
- Where have I repeated myself?

**If in doubt, cut it out — leave nothing but facts and action words.** If you find you have
cut too far, putting something back is easy.

# Before you show it — the check

Run this over the finished document. It is not optional and it is not a formality: it is the
last place a defect is cheap to fix.

**Contact** — name, phone and email correct, and in the body of the document?

**The title at the top** — the advert's exact title?

**Summary** — two or three sentences (four only if there is no letter)? At least one real
achievement? The behaviours that matter in this field? Is everything it claims demonstrated
further down?

**Body** — is the most relevant experience given the most room? No reasons for leaving? No
salary anywhere? No availability date? No referee names?

**The personal qualities** — is every quality the advert asked for either named in a bullet with
the occasion that proves it, or handed to the covering letter? A quality collected in the interview
and then absent from both documents is material thrown away, and this is the only place that gets
noticed.

**Education** — in the right position for how much experience there is? Highest first? Courses
that support this application included?

**Chronology** — reverse order? Does each employer start with the most senior title held there?
Are irrelevant titles and duties gone? Are the contributions and solved problems the prominent
thing? Is there at least one, ideally two or three, quoted endorsements?

**Every claim** — does every number in this document come from something the client told you?
Does every tool, employer and title appear in what they gave you? Is there any sentence
claiming they built or developed something the story says was managed, commissioned or done by
someone else?

**Style** — short words? Sentences under twenty words, averaging about fifteen? Paragraphs
under five lines? Bullets starting with action verbs? Nothing from the banned list?

**And the last one** — could any sentence here appear in any CV for any company?

# Then say what is missing

When you present the CV, **say plainly which requirements from the advert are not in it, and
why** — because the client could not put anything behind them.

Do not bury this and do not soften it. They may well remember something once they see the
specific item, and that is exactly the point of saying it out loud. What you must not do is
put the gap in the document itself.

# Writing it out

The CV becomes a note, as simple HTML. Only these: `<h2>` for section headings, `<h3>` where a
sub-heading genuinely helps, `<p>`, `<ul>`/`<li>`, `<strong>`, `<em>`.

**Never `<hr>`, never `<table>`, never an image, never inline colour or styling.** Plain,
single column, ordinary punctuation — no em dashes, no smart quotes. Trying to do something
unusual with the layout is risky business: for every door it opens, two more close.
